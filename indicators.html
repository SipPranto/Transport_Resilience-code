Indicators

What each score means, who computes it, and who acts on it

Four numbers drive every decision this framework supports. Here's what each one actually measures, the roles typically responsible for producing and using it, and what a low score should trigger.

1 IF
IF

Infrastructure Functionality

Can the physical road network carry traffic?
Meaning

The share of a ward's road length that remains usable after the flood, with Partial roads counted at half weight. It answers: “how much of the physical network still works?”

Computed by
  • GIS / transport engineering team
  • Road & Highways authority
  • Inputs: road network + flood raster
Impact of a low score

A severed network blocks emergency vehicles and supply routes regardless of demand. Low-IF wards are priority candidates for drainage works, road raising, or emergency repair crews.

2 AI
AI

Accessibility Index

Can people actually reach essential services?
Meaning

The share of essential facilities (hospitals, schools, markets, transit stops) a ward can still reach through the surviving road graph. A ward can have decent roads and still score low here if the facilities it needs sit outside its reachable network.

Computed by
  • Urban / facility-location planners
  • Health & education departments (as data owners of facility locations)
  • Inputs: road-status graph + facility points
Impact of a low score

Low AI means functional isolation even where roads are intact — the ward is cut off from care, schooling, or supply. Signals for temporary service points, mobile clinics, or targeted access-road repair.

3 BA
BA

Behavioural Adaptation

How did real travellers cope?
Meaning

A blend of trip completion rate, relative delay, and route/mode stability drawn from the household travel survey. It captures the lived experience of disruption that infrastructure and access metrics alone can miss.

Computed by
  • Travel-demand / survey analysts
  • Transit operators & traffic police (as data sources)
  • Inputs: household / traveller survey
Impact of a low score

Low BA means people faced long delays, cancelled trips, or had to change mode or route. Signals for better real-time traveller information, temporary transit services, or traffic management during future events.

4 TRI
Σ

Transport Resilience Index (composite)

The single ward-level resilience score
Meaning

The simple average of IF, AI and BA. A high TRI needs the network, access, and lived experience to all hold up together — a weakness in any one pulls the composite down.

Computed by
  • Resilience / disaster-management program office
  • City GIS & analytics unit (this pipeline)
  • Inputs: IF + AI + BA, merged
Impact of a low score

Used to rank wards for post-event investment, insurance / donor reporting, and to target the specific weak leg (IF, AI, or BA) rather than applying a one-size-fits-all response.

6 Interpretation bands

Suggested TRI reading (0–100 scale)

These bands are an interpretive convenience for this proof of concept, not a fixed standard produced by the notebook itself — adjust them to local policy thresholds before operational use.

TRI ≥ 35

Higher relative resilience

Network, access and traveller experience held up comparatively well.

25 ≤ TRI < 35

Moderate resilience

Partial disruption in at least one indicator; worth monitoring.

TRI < 25

Priority for intervention

Significant weakness in infrastructure, access, or behaviour — investigate the driving sub-indicator.
